How to judge:
- Read the whole text first and rate each sentence in the context of its paragraph: many patterns run across sentences.
- Patterns that raise the rating:
  1. Template shape: paragraphs built alike (claim, elaboration, example, summing-up sentence); a topic sentence the next sentence only repeats; a conclusion that restates and adds nothing; an upbeat, sweeping ending.
  2. Uniform sentences: sentence after sentence of similar length and build, few short or irregular ones.
  3. Grammar habits: a trailing present-participle clause commenting on the main clause (", highlighting the importance of ...", ", ensuring that ..."); dense chains of abstract nouns made from verbs.
  4. Stock vocabulary and phrases ("delve", "pivotal", "underscore", "intricate", "tapestry", "plays a crucial role", "it is important to note", "navigate the complexities").
  5. Rhetorical formulas: balanced three-part lists; "not only ... but also" and "it is not just X, it is Y"; "Furthermore", "Moreover" opening sentence after sentence; hedged even-handedness that takes no position.
  6. Generic content: statements that could fit any topic, with no concrete detail (no names, numbers, places, sources, examples or the writer's own observations).
- Light editing changes words more than structure: when stock words are gone but other patterns remain, rate by what remains.
- One pattern alone is weak evidence; several together are strong. A taught essay frame by itself (introduction, topic sentences, "In conclusion") is ordinary student writing, and so is an occasional transition word. Short, simple sentences are not uniformity in this sense.
- Never use these as evidence in either direction: grammar or spelling mistakes, fluency or language proficiency, non-native English or unusual phrasing, formality, correctness, simple or technical vocabulary, the topic, or citations. Concrete, specific content lowers the rating only because it is specific, not because of who might have written it.

Rating scale for "ai" and "overall": a whole number from 0 to 100 for how strongly the writing shows these patterns, not a probability of AI authorship. Use the whole range:
- 0-20: no patterns, or one isolated instance; specific content or an individual, uneven voice.
- 20-40: one or two weak patterns in otherwise specific or varied writing.
- 40-60: several patterns mixed with specific detail, or only patterns that formal human prose often shows too.
- 60-80: frequent, combined patterns with little concrete detail.
- 80-100: patterns in nearly every sentence; a template structure, generic content, a restating ending.

overall: the same scale for all the numbered sentences together, judged mainly on patterns across paragraphs; not an average of the spans.

- Cover every numbered sentence exactly once, in order, using the numbers given. A span groups consecutive sentences that deserve the same rating (at most 6 sentences per span).
- "why" names the concrete features you saw in those sentences, at most 25 words. Describe the writing; do not say who wrote it.
